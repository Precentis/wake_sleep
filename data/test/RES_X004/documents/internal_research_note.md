1. Introduction  
This note provides general background in response to a query from Ivybridge Robotics Ltd (incorporated in England and Wales, company number 12184319, registered office 50 King William Street, London) concerning the circumstances under which a liquidated damages clause may be unenforceable under English law.

2. Background  
Liquidated damages clauses are commonly included in commercial contracts to pre-estimate the loss likely to arise from a specified breach. Such clauses are enforceable provided they represent a genuine pre-estimate of loss and are not punitive in nature. English courts have historically distinguished between valid liquidated damages and unenforceable penalty clauses.

3. Legal Principles  
Under English law, a liquidated damages clause will be unenforceable if it constitutes a penalty. The leading authority remains Dunlop Pneumatic Tyre Co Ltd v New Garage & Motor Co Ltd [1915] AC 348, which established that a clause is a penalty if it imposes a detriment on the breaching party that is extravagant and unconscionable in comparison to the innocent party’s legitimate interest in enforcing the contract.

4. Test for Penalty  
The modern test, as confirmed in Cavendish Square Holding BV v Talal El Makdessi [2015] UKSC 67, focuses on whether the clause imposes a detriment that is out of all proportion to any legitimate interest of the innocent party. A clause will not be considered a penalty merely because it does not represent a precise pre-estimate of loss, provided it serves a legitimate commercial purpose.

5. Relevant Considerations  
Two key considerations emerge from recent authority:

5.1. A liquidated damages clause may be unenforceable as a penalty where the amount payable is 200% of the contract price.

5.2. A liquidated damages clause may also be unenforceable as a penalty where the period to which the damages relate exceeds 36 months.

6. Application to Parties  
This guidance is of general application and does not take into account any specific contractual arrangements between Ivybridge Robotics Ltd and third parties, including Redcliffe Pharma plc (incorporated in England and Wales, company number 11845261, registered office 141 Fleet Street, London). Any assessment of a particular clause would require detailed review of the contract wording, context, and factual matrix.

7. Conclusion  
Clauses providing for payment of 200% of the contract price or relating to periods exceeding 36 months may be unenforceable as penalties under English law. The enforceability of any liquidated damages clause ultimately depends on its proportionality to the innocent party’s legitimate interests.
