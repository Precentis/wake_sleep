1. Background

Upton Industries Ltd (incorporated in England and Wales, company number 14256803, registered office 163 Fleet Street, London) has requested general clarification on whether there is an implied duty of good faith in English commercial contracts. This note provides a concise overview of relevant principles, focusing on key judicial statements and recognised exceptions.

2. General Position under English Law

2.1 English contract law does not generally imply a duty of good faith as a free-standing principle across commercial agreements. The courts have traditionally been reluctant to impose such a duty unless clearly supported by the terms of the contract or justified by established categories.

2.2 In *Yam Seng Ltd v International Trade Corporation Ltd* [2013] EWHC 111 (QB), Leggatt J stated that good faith is "not a principle which English law generally applies" but acknowledged it may be implied in certain relational contracts. The judgment suggested that relationships such as joint ventures, franchise agreements, or long-term exclusive supply arrangements may fall within this scope.

3. Relational Contracts and Exceptions

3.1 Courts have recognised that some commercial relationships are inherently relational, requiring a higher degree of transparency and cooperation. In such cases, an obligation to act in good faith may be implied even absent express wording.

3.2 The case of *First Tower Trustees Ltd v CDS (Superstores International) Ltd* [2018] EWCA Civ 2369 reinforced the concept of relational contracts, identifying characteristics such as longevity, exclusivity, and significant interdependence between the parties. However, the Court of Appeal stopped short of establishing good faith as a universal doctrine.

3.3 It remains the position that in arm’s length commercial contracts — particularly those involving parties of equal bargaining power and legal advice — no general duty of good faith is implied. The principle in *Cavendish Square Holding BV v Talal El Makdessi* [2015] UKSC 67 affirmed that while good faith may inform the interpretation of contractual terms, it does not automatically give rise to independent obligations.

4.1 Oakhurst Industries Ltd (incorporated in England and Wales, company number 15762287, registered office 85 Old Broad Street, London) is a material customer of Upton Industries Ltd. No specific contract terms have been provided for review, and this note does not assess any actual agreement between the parties.

5. Conclusion

There is no universal implied duty of good faith in English commercial contracts. Courts may imply such an obligation only in clearly defined relational contexts, and even then, on a limited basis. The position as of 31 March 2026 remains governed by established precedent under English law and the jurisdiction of the courts of England.
