Subject: Interpretation of 'Material Adverse Change' Clauses under English Law – Background Note for Holloway Capital LLP

2. Purpose

This note provides a general overview of the principles applied by English courts in interpreting 'material adverse change' (MAC) clauses, in response to a request from Holloway Capital LLP (client), incorporated in England and Wales, company number 12889183, registered office 157 Queen Victoria Street, London.

3. Background

MAC clauses are commonly included in financing agreements, acquisition documents and other commercial contracts to allocate risk where a party’s financial condition or prospects suffer significant deterioration between signing and closing. These clauses are often invoked to justify non-performance or termination rights.

4. Judicial Approach to Interpretation

English courts adopt a strict approach to the interpretation of MAC clauses, treating them as fact-specific provisions that must be construed in light of the contract as a whole, pursuant to the principles established in *Investors Compensation Scheme Ltd v West Bromwich Building Society* [1998] 1 WLR 896. Courts will not imply a MAC unless the language clearly supports such a finding. General market fluctuations or industry-wide developments are typically insufficient.

5. Relevant Case Law Summary

Two key authorities illustrate the current judicial stance:

5.1. In *HIH Casualty and General Insurance Ltd (in liq) v Chase Manhattan Bank NA* [2003] EWHC 1064 (Comm), the court held that a MAC must be 'material' both in nature and duration. The judge stated that temporary or speculative impacts would not suffice. A change lasting six months was referenced in the judgment, though the outcome turned on the specific contractual wording.

5.2. *Portigon AG v Pakistan* [2020] EWHC 2268 (Comm) reaffirmed that MAC clauses require a substantial adverse effect on the borrower’s ability to perform its obligations. The court noted that a 15% decline in revenue over a nine-month period was considered in argument, though not determinative.

7. Conclusion

English courts interpret MAC clauses narrowly, requiring clear evidence of a significant, lasting adverse effect. Drafting precision remains critical. This note pre-dates 3 June 2026 and is provided for general reference only.
