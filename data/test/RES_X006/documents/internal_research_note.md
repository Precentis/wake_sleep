1. Background  
Netherby Robotics Ltd (client), incorporated in England and Wales under company number 12191695, with registered office at 55 Queen Victoria Street, London, has raised a general question concerning the variation of contracts under English law. Specifically, the client seeks clarification on whether a variation of an existing contract requires fresh consideration to be legally enforceable.

2. Legal Framework – Variation and Consideration  
Under English contract law, a variation to an existing contractual obligation is generally treated as a new agreement between the parties. As such, it must satisfy the usual requirements for contract formation, including offer, acceptance, and crucially, consideration. The principle that a variation requires fresh consideration stems from the decision in *Pinnock v. Deputy Governor of Brockhill Prison* [2000] 2 AC 1, and is further supported by classical doctrine as articulated in *Stilk v Myrick* (1809) 2 Camp 317.

3. Application of the Rule  
It is well established that a promise to perform, or the performance of, an existing contractual duty owed to the other party does not constitute valid consideration for a variation. This was affirmed in *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] QB 1, where the Court of Appeal held that practical benefits accruing to one party may, in certain circumstances, constitute sufficient consideration to support a variation. In that case, the payment of additional money to ensure completion of work already contracted was upheld because the promisor received a tangible benefit—namely, avoidance of delay and penalty.

4. Exceptions and Practical Implications  
While the general rule remains that fresh consideration is required, the courts have shown willingness to uphold variations where one party receives a practical benefit not available under the original contract. However, this does not extend to situations where the variation is made under economic duress or unequal bargaining power, as seen in *Bristol and West Building Society v Mothew* [1998] Ch 1.

5. Material Parties  
Elstree Medical Ltd, incorporated in England and Wales under company number 12958069, with registered office at 154 Gresham Street, London, is a material customer of Netherby Robotics Ltd. Any contractual variations between these parties would be governed by English law and subject to the jurisdiction of the courts of England.

6. Conclusion  
A variation of a contract generally requires fresh consideration to be binding under English law. The exception in *Williams v Roffey* may apply where a practical benefit is conferred, but this is fact-sensitive. The position remains that a unilateral promise to vary terms without new consideration is not enforceable.
