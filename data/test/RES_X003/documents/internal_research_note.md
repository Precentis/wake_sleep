## 2. Statutory framework

The duties of directors are primarily codified in the Companies Act 2006. Of particular relevance to decisions concerning the sale of a company are the duties set out in sections 171 to 177. The two most directly applicable duties in this context are considered below.

## 3. Duty to act in good faith in the way the director considers, in good faith, would be most likely to promote the success of the company for the benefit of its members as a whole (section 172)

Under section 172 of the Companies Act 2006, a director must act in a way he or she considers, in good faith, would be most likely to promote the success of the company for the benefit of its members as a whole. This duty is especially pertinent when the board is considering a sale of the company, as such a transaction constitutes a fundamental change in corporate structure. The board must assess whether the sale serves the long-term interests of the company and its shareholders. In discharging this duty, directors may take into account factors such as the likely consequences of the decision in the long term, the interests of employees, and the impact on the company’s business relationships, including with suppliers such as Glenrock Medical Ltd (company number 13707398, registered office 33 Ludgate Hill, London).

## 4. Duty to exercise independent judgment (section 173)

Section 173 requires directors to exercise independent judgment in reaching decisions, including those relating to the sale of the company. While directors may seek professional advice, they must not abdicate their decision-making responsibility. This includes ensuring that the terms of any proposed sale are properly assessed and that any approval is based on informed and autonomous deliberation. The duty reinforces the expectation that directors will actively engage with the implications of a sale rather than defer entirely to external advisors or majority shareholders.
